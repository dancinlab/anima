SUMMARY — Lane M: "EEG-grown CLM via mitosis" (TOY · CPU · $0)
==============================================================
A NEW LANE for anima — the GRADIENT-FREE GROWTH lane. Distinct from
Lane A (AKIDA on-chip), Lane G (forge GPU CE-descent), Lane P (py+CUDA
gradient). Recorded SEPARATELY per a_lane_akida_gpu_split.

METHOD: empty CLM (1 seed cell, 0 trained weights) -> 5-ch tension-link
[alpha,theta,gamma,1-delta,beta] carrying a deterministic synthetic-EEG
stream -> gamma>0.20 triggers MITOSIS split (EEG L12) -> daughter cell
specializes toward the EEG drive at that moment + SPLIT_NOISE_FLOOR=0.1
symmetry break. The grown topology IS the "recording". NO gradient, NO CE
loss, NO backprop anywhere (p8: inference mitosis IS the learning).
3 seeds [1,2,3]; mean +- seed-std.

BOTTOM LINE (brutally honest, p7 — NOT CE/perplexity):
  Gradient-free mitosis growth from a 5-ch EEG tension-link DOES write a
  bounded, stable structure that reconstructs the EEG's marginal band-power
  distribution well above an un-grown floor (M1 HOLDS, M3 HOLDS). But it does
  NOT record the EEG's TEMPORAL DYNAMICS beyond seed-noise: a phase-shuffled
  EEG (identical marginals) produces an effectively equal recording, and
  stage-decode is at chance (M2 INCONCLUSIVE, leaning closed-negative). So at
  this toy scale the answer to "does EEG-driven mitosis record the EEG into
  structure, beating random + shuffled controls?" is: it records the
  DISTRIBUTION, not the DYNAMICS — the dynamics claim is WITHIN NOISE.
  A valid negative-leaning finding (a_paper_negative_ok): EEG-driven growth
  at toy scale is not distinguishable from distribution-matched growth on the
  dynamics axis.

SCOPE (a_toy_scale_recheck / a_scale_honest_scope): TOY ONLY — synthetic EEG
(NOT a real headset), small cell budget (MAX_CELLS=64), CPU, $0. Toy->prod
and synthetic->real-EEG transfer UNVERIFIED. "Record MY EEG" (real user
headset) is a HUMAN-GATED follow-up, NOT done here.
