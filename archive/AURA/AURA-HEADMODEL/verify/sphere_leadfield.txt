VERDICT — 🟡 SUPPORTED-NUMERICAL (toy, sphere ≠ real head but physical > gaussian)

C10 STRAWMAN RULING:  ✅ C10 SUPPORTED (electrode saturation IS a gaussian artifact).
  In the SAME harness, the 3-shell physical LF does NOT saturate — R2 keeps
  climbing with more electrodes (0.189→0.401, the high-M deltas stay POSITIVE:
  +0.075 @256, +0.093 @1024). The gaussian-blur LF climbs only weakly and
  flattens (0.136→0.218, delta collapses to +0.022 @1024). The physical model
  behaves like C10's exponential/power-law kernels (un-saturating), NOT like
  the gaussian strawman of C5. → C10's "saturation = gaussian-only artifact"
  is CONFIRMED by a physically-grounded (non-gaussian-assumption) model.
  Practical corollary: under a real head model, adding scalp electrodes still
  helps — the C5 electrode-saturation pessimism was a kernel-shape artifact.

DEPTH (Q1):  qualitative MATCH with gaussian toy C15. 3-shell shows monotone
  collapse cortex→deep (0.295→0.197). Same DIRECTION as C15 (0.82→0.10). The
  absolute decay is much GENTLER here (×1.5 vs C15's ×8) — toy-specific; the
  C15 3D-grid gaussian over-attenuates depth vs the analytic 1/r sphere. Both
  agree QUALITATIVELY: deeper source → worse recovery. (a finding: the toy
  may OVERSTATE the depth wall steepness, though direction is robust.)

SKULL RATIO (Q3):  NOT-DISCRIMINATING over 1/20–1/80.  R2 = 0.316 for all three
  ratios; the LPF cutoff stays n≈19 (set by source eccentricity geometry, not
  the conductivity contrast in this range). HONEST: over the physiological
  1/20–1/80 window the skull ratio does not move recovery in this toy. We do
  NOT claim a skull-ratio dependence — it is measured FLAT. (Transfer-matrix
  cross-check v4 confirmed flatness is genuine, not a normalization artifact.)

NOT-MEASURED (honest):
  - real (non-spherical) head geometry — sphere ≠ skull foramina/anisotropy.
  - tangential dipoles (only radial modeled here; real cortex has both).
  - σ_skull anisotropy (radial≠tangential conductivity — known real effect).
  - absolute R2 calibration vs a measured EEG inverse benchmark.
