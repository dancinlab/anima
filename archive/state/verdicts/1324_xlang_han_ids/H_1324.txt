WHAT THIS ESTABLISHES (vs the H_1318 artifact)
  H_1318's Han rows (zh −1.481, ja −1.230) were partly a DECOMPOSITION ARTIFACT: the
  full-char-residual blew the vocab to ~9.5k symbols → the tiny unigram head fragmented.
  H_1324 REMOVES that artifact (modest vocab 2116/1582, no residual) and the harm
  HALVES (zh −1.481→−0.737, ja −1.230→−0.628) — confirming part of H_1318's negative
  WAS the bad decomposition. BUT the gain does NOT cross zero: even with a clean,
  real, recoverable IDS component decomposition, Han STRUCT CE stays ABOVE RAW CE AND
  does not beat its component-shuffle control. So Han (logographic) composition
  GENUINELY does NOT help this gradient-free per-cell-unigram mechanism — a REAL
  result, not the H_1318 artifact.

  WHY (diagnosable from the controls, c9): unlike Hangul (67 jamo, a TINY shared
  alphabet where the L/V/T composition is highly regular and the per-cell unigram head
  can exploit it → +0.216, shuffle-earned +0.088), Han decomposes into a LARGE
  component inventory (2116/1582) with IRREGULAR, position-dependent, many-component
  structure — the average Han char → ~2 components drawn from ~2000, so the
  component-bigram statistics the CTX=4 Voronoi-unigram head can capture are too sparse
  to beat the raw-byte ceiling, and the component-shuffle barely changes the score
  (Δ-vs-shuffle ≈ 0) → the component identity carries almost no next-unit signal at
  this substrate scale. The Hangul win rests on a SMALL, REGULAR compositional alphabet;
  Han's compositionality is real but TOO HIGH-CARDINALITY / IRREGULAR for this mechanism.
