WHY THOSE TESTS MIGHT NOT REJECT THE BIOLOGICAL ROLE (a_break_the_wall,
a_no_llm_frame_trap, c15): every prior adaptive arm used a SINGLE
timescale — one EMA, one buffer, one gain law. Real neuromodulators are
DUAL-timescale: dopamine has a slow TONIC baseline (sets the operating
mode over minutes) PLUS fast PHASIC bursts (reward-prediction-error
transients over ~100ms); norepinephrine has tonic (arousal mode) vs
phasic (salience) modes (Aston-Jones/Cohen). A single-EMA controller
CANNOT simultaneously hold a slow regime baseline AND respond to a fast
transient — it is forced to pick one bandwidth, which the grid-tuned
fixed point already matches. The genuinely different family: DECOMPOSE
the modulator into TWO timescales (slow tonic EMA + fast phasic
transient) and ask whether the two-timescale controller beats best-fixed
ACROSS REGIMES where a single-timescale one (H_1284 arm B) cannot.

TWO EMAs with DIFFERENT time-constants (pre-registered BEFORE scoring):

  PHASIC (fast): phasic_t = (1 − α_phasic)·phasic_{t−1} + α_phasic·s_t
                 α_phasic = 0.50  (fast transient ≈ 2-tick horizon)
  phasic TRANSIENT = phasic_t − tonic_t  (the fast deviation FROM the
                 slow baseline — a reward-prediction-error-style burst).
Knob map (gain_t = tonic_slow_baseline + phasic_fast_transient), acting
on the SAME knobs H_1284 modulates (so any lift is the timescale split,
not a new lever):
  ACh plasticity LR(t) = clip(LR0·(1 + kT_tonic·(tonic_t − TH0)
                                      + kP_phasic·(phasic_t − tonic_t)),

     (slow tonic raises baseline plasticity in a sustained-surprise
      regime; fast phasic burst spikes plasticity on a genuinely novel
      event then decays — the decomposition a single EMA cannot do.)
