RIDGE-COHERENCE METRIC (reported explicitly — the 2-D analogue of the 1-D peak-count)
  For an arm, take the per-edge discrimination field. Define the RIDGE = the top
  RIDGE_FRAC (=0.20) fraction of edges by discrimination. Two quantities:
    (i)  RIDGE-ALIGN(arm, boundary) = mean over ridge edges of [1 - dist(edge_midpoint,
         boundary_curve)/D_MAX], i.e. how close the high-discrimination edges sit to the
         GIVEN boundary curve (1 = exactly on it, 0 = max distance D_MAX=sqrt(2)/2 away).
         dist(point, curve) computed against a dense sampling of each boundary curve.
    (ii) RIDGE-COHERENCE(arm) = the LARGEST-connected-component fraction of the ridge
         edge-set under 4-neighbour grid adjacency: (#edges in the largest connected
         ridge cluster) / (#ridge edges). A real boundary ridge is ONE connected curve
         (coherence -> 1); a shuffle ridge is scattered specks (coherence low, many tiny
         components). This is the 2-D generalization of "1 coherent peak vs many spikes".
