F-PERAXIS-SHUFFLE — each axis independent & meaningful (the #1765 non-tautology control)
=========================================================================================
Harness: UNIVERSE/kosmos_dim_ladder.py  (STANDALONE, CPU, $0, 3 seeds)
At full D=8, shuffle the coordinate columns of ONE axis alone (random per-anchor
permutation) and measure the recovery DROP for:
  own_drop    = recovery of that axis's OWN attribute (should DROP a lot — the
                axis is meaningful and carries that attribute).
  cross_drop  = mean recovery of OTHER axes' attributes (should be ~0 — the axes
                are INDEPENDENT; corrupting one does not corrupt the others).
  target_drop = drop in the held-out joint-target recovery.
This generalises the #1765 single-axis shuffle control to a per-axis matrix and
is the NON-TAUTOLOGICAL evidence: there is no shared monotone index, so each
axis's information is isolated.

VERDICT (F-PERAXIS-SHUFFLE): INDEPENDENCE HOLDS for every axis.
  For ALL 7 axes, own_drop (range +0.111 .. +0.438) >> cross_drop (range
  -0.0001 .. +0.034). Shuffling axis-k degrades recovery of attribute-k (own)
  but barely touches the other attributes (cross ~0). Each axis carries its OWN
  isolated signal — NOT a monotone function of one shared index (resolves the
  #1765 tautology caveat).

  NOTE on the redundant axes: s and kappa DO carry their own attribute
  (own_drop ~0.24-0.25, same as the independent axes) — their redundancy is NOT
  that they are meaningless, but that their ATTRIBUTE duplicates another axis's
  (s.lab_s ~= t.lab_t, kap.lab_kap ~= e.lab_e). That is exactly why they add
  no NEW joint-target info (F-NEWINFO SATURATED) yet shuffle-degrade their own
  attribute. Independence (isolated info) and redundancy (duplicated signal)
  are distinct and both correctly measured.
