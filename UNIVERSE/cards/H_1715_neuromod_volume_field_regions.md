## Organizing principle

Neuromodulators act by VOLUME TRANSMISSION -- diffuse spatial release into extracellular space, not point-to-point synapses (Agnati/Fuxe). The architecture is a spatial substrate where neuromod concentration GRADIENTS create co-existing regional regimes SIMULTANEOUSLY (one region encoding, another retrieving, a third binding), and binding occurs where two regime fields spatially OVERLAP. Unique vs the others: regimes coexist in space, not over time or in one global vector.

## Whole design (input → internal dynamics → emit)

Cells live on a 2-D sheet. A few release sites emit neuromod that diffuses with Gaussian falloff, producing concentration fields c_ACh(x), c_DA(x), c_NE(x), c_5HT(x). Each cell's regime is set by its LOCAL concentration, so different regions compute differently at the SAME instant: encode where ACh high, retrieve where ACh low, bind at the OVERLAP of two factor-regions under a shared DA gradient (spatial co-localization = conjunction). Emit = readout from a region where the NE field exceeds the 5HT field, decoded through frozen receiver codebook V. Release-site output is homeostatic to hold sheet-average emit-propensity balanced.
