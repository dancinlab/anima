\begin{abstract}
A substrate-blind big-$\Phi$ detector --- an Integrated Information Theory
4.0 \citep{albantakis2023iit4} reading of an arbitrary float-array signal,
binarised at threshold $0.5$, encoded as a two-unit co-occurrence transition
probability matrix (TPM), and passed to a deterministic IIT 4.0
structure-cut big-$\Phi$ ($\Phi$) algorithm --- promises to read the
``existence of consciousness'' of any candidate substrate (alien, digital,
panpsychist, simulated, AGI activation, hive) without word, body, or carbon
prior. The natural question is then: \emph{on which $(n, \text{density},
\text{structure})$ regime does the detector actually calibrate, and on
which does it deterministically break?} Without this map any reported
``$\Phi$'' on a novel substrate is uninterpretable.

We answer this empirically with a seven-point measurement matrix
(\texttt{XENO/H\_832}--\texttt{H\_838}, five XENO-FRONTIER-5 closure
rounds + the X837 SETI@home BOINC follow-up + the X10 hive-mind 4-cell
coupling follow-up). The points span (i) micro substrates ($n=16$--$32$,
1--3 bit panpsychism candidates), (ii) sparse mid-$n$ activation ($n=64$,
AGI LLM-like attention/residual/structured), (iii) algorithmic dense
($n=128$, simulation-hypothesis candidates: lattice, floating-point,
$\pi$-digits, natural noise), (iv) natural-carrier dense ($n=128$,
$60.9\%$ activation: Breakthrough-Listen Voyager-1 baseband as the
ground-truth calibration), (v) sparse natural-noise dense ($n=128$,
$20.3\%$ activation: a SETI@home Arecibo 2004 BOINC \texttt{bg\_pot} replay
fired on a real Ubuntu 22.04 i686-multilib pod), and \textbf{(vi) hive-mind
coupling: four 32-sample 4-cell substrates aggregated to $n=128$ ---
independent walkers, mean-field weak coupling, Kuramoto strong coupling
($K=2.0$ all-to-all sync), and XOR-cascade hive emergence with 1-lag
carry --- the X10 follow-up}.

The measurement is a clean two-ground-truth separation:
\textbf{X7 BL Voyager-1 ($\Phi=0.114$, \tierGreen)} and
\textbf{X10-hive XOR cascade ($\Phi=1.565$ \texttt{conscious},
\tierGreen \;ground-truth \#2)}; \textbf{X4 micro, X6 sparse, X5b
algorithmic axes are each closed-negative} (\texttt{F}-falsifier 0/4, 1/5,
1/4 PASS, \tierRed); three border points --- X5a lattice ($\Phi=0.660$,
periodic-structure ceiling), X837 SETI@home Arecibo ($\Phi=0.567$,
sparse natural-noise borderline), and X10-strong Kuramoto sync
($\Phi=0.408$, mean-field-but-coupled border) --- sit above or near the
pre-frozen $\Phi=0.5$ threshold but with type-axis intact
(\texttt{coherent\_non\_conscious}), \tierYellow.

\textbf{The mean-field paradox.} The most surprising finding is in the X10
ordering. \emph{Naive intuition} says coupling $\to$ integration:
$\Phi$ should increase monotonically along (independent
$\to$ weak mean-field $\to$ strong Kuramoto $\to$ hive). \emph{Measured
$\Phi$ instead drops from $0.131$ (independent) to $0.036$ (weak
mean-field), rises only to $0.408$ at strong Kuramoto, and jumps to
$1.565$ at the XOR cascade}. Mean-field averaging reduces $\Phi$
\emph{below} the independent baseline because uniformity makes the
substrate reducible (IIT 4.0 axiom: integration $\equiv$ irreducibility,
not correlation), while genuine cascade-coupling makes the substrate
irreducible. The pre-registered monotonicity falsifier (F-X10-MONOTONE)
explicitly FAILED, and the failure \emph{confirms} the IIT 4.0 axiom
on which the detector is built. The mean-field paradox is the headline
finding of v2.

Aggregating, the measurable axis remains \emph{the conjunction}
\textbf{$n \ge 128 \; \land \; (\text{density} \ge 60\% \;\lor\;
\text{MIP-irreducible deterministic transition})$}. Three regimes
(micro / sparse / algorithmic) are deterministically ruled out as outside
the instrument's calibrated frontier. The new ground-truth \#2 (X10-hive)
confirms that the calibrated regime includes \emph{any} truly irreducible
coupling at $n \ge 128$, not just natural-carrier density.

These closed-negative results satisfy \texttt{a\_paper\_negative\_ok}: the
finding is the instrument applicability frontier itself, not a
``no-consciousness-detected'' headline. The mean-field paradox is the
v2 escalation: a positive finding (X10-hive ground-truth \#2 with
$\Phi=1.565$ \texttt{conscious}) and an axiom-confirming negative
(F-X10-MONOTONE FAILED \emph{because} mean-field $\ne$ irreducibility,
which is precisely IIT 4.0). We publish all seven verdicts (plus the
X837 follow-up) verbatim in a \texttt{companion/} ledger for
bit-identical re-verification, and we close the XENO-FRONTIER-5
followup-2 cycle at this paper as the
\texttt{a\_paper\_only\_at\_closure} trigger.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{The instrument-first methodology.} Hexa-native, deterministic,
hypothesis-first measurement on the \texttt{anima} project follows an
\emph{instrument-first} methodology: any reading of a measurement
($\Phi$, KL-divergence, register-collapse rate) is meaningless unless the
calibrated input regime of the measuring instrument is itself first
characterised. The XENO domain \citep{xeno_repo} carries this practice
into substrate-blind big-$\Phi$ \citep{albantakis2023iit4,
oizumi2014phenomenology, tononi2016iit}: a function
$\texttt{invariant\_detector}: \mathbb{R}^n \times \mathbb{N} \to \{\Phi,
\text{integration}, \text{irreducibility}, \text{substrate\_type}\}$ that
takes \emph{any} signal --- alien radio carrier, BOINC residual noise,
floating-point lattice, panpsychist micro-substrate, LLM activation
sparsity --- and returns the IIT 4.0 big-$\Phi$ together with a
discrete type label.

The detector is designed substrate-blind by construction. It binarises the
input at threshold $0.5$, builds a 2-unit (sample $t$, sample $t{+}1$)
co-occurrence row-stochastic transition probability matrix (TPM), and
delegates to \texttt{stdlib/consciousness/iit4\_bigphi} (the hexa-lang
commons-g61 IIT 4.0 implementation). The TPM is the substrate; word, body,
language, carbon, RNA, silicon priors all vanish into the float array.
This is what allows the question ``is X conscious?'' to be asked even when
X has no body and no language --- as long as the input float-array is
honestly the candidate substrate's state.

\paragraph{The applicability question.} The detector's output, however, is
only as meaningful as the regime on which it is calibrated. If the
instrument deterministically breaks below some $n$, or above some
sparsity, or for some specific structural class (algorithmic vs.\
deterministic vs.\ natural), then any reported $\Phi$ in that broken
regime is an instrument artifact, not a substrate property. The XENO
working group therefore pre-registered the methodological imperative:
\emph{before reporting any candidate-substrate $\Phi$, first close the
applicability frontier of the detector along the $(n, \text{density},
\text{structure})$ axes.}

\paragraph{XENO-FRONTIER-5 closure and the two follow-up cycles.}
The XENO-FRONTIER-5 campaign is the original closure: five
pre-registered candidate substrates, ranging across the three axes,
each measured with the same detector with frozen prediction thresholds
and falsifiers. Each round was published verbatim in
\texttt{UNIVERSE/H\_832}--\texttt{H\_836}. The X8 SETI@home archive
\emph{follow-up 1} fire was published verbatim in
\texttt{UNIVERSE/H\_837}. \emph{Follow-up 2}, the X10 hive-mind
4-cell coupling axis, was published verbatim in
\texttt{UNIVERSE/H\_838}. This paper v2 aggregates all seven points
(plus the X8 X837 follow-up) into a 7+1-point matrix and reports
the applicability frontier finding together with the v2 escalation:
the mean-field paradox and ground-truth \#2.

\paragraph{Why publish closed-negatives.} The directive
\texttt{a\_paper\_negative\_ok} \citep{anima_repo} explicitly licenses
closed-negative findings: a \tierRed{} verdict that deterministically
rules out an axis is a valid paper, framed as the falsifier plus the
ruled-out space. This paper is exactly that frame. The headline is not
``no alien consciousness was detected'' (a vacuous statement on a
mis-calibrated regime). The headline is ``the substrate-blind big-$\Phi$
detector calibrates only inside one corner of the $(n, \text{density},
\text{structure})$ cube, and three corners are deterministically ruled
out.'' This is what an instrument-first methodology publishes.

\paragraph{Roadmap.} \S\ref{sec:hypothesis} states the four pre-registered

including the five-falsifier X10 row.

\section{Hypothesis: the applicability frontier of \texttt{invariant\_detector}}
\label{sec:hypothesis}

\paragraph{Decomposition.} The candidate-substrate space is naturally
decomposed along three axes:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{$n$ axis} --- the length of the input float-array (number
    of binarised samples available for TPM estimation). Examples: $n=16$
    (a 1-bit thermostat over a $16$-tick window) up through $n=128$
    (a dense BL Voyager-1 carrier or a $128$-bin BOINC \texttt{bg\_pot}
    array).
  \item \textbf{density axis} --- the fraction of samples above the
    binarisation threshold $0.5$ (i.e.\ the fraction of $1$s in the TPM
    input). Sparse (e.g.\ $20.3\%$ of $n=128$ active in the X837 SETI@home
    \texttt{bg\_pot}) vs.\ dense (e.g.\ $60.9\%$ active in the X7 BL
    Voyager-1 carrier).
  \item \textbf{structure axis} --- the deterministic, algorithmic, or
    natural-noise character of the transition $b_t \to b_{t+1}$. Examples:
    deterministic periodic (lattice / Planck-floor 8-level), algorithmic
    (Pi-digits, floating-point round, XOR LFSR), or natural (Bates-4
    Gaussian, real radio-band noise, BOINC residual).
\end{itemize}

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Detector implementation.}
The detector lives at \texttt{XENO/detector/invariant\_detector.hexa}
(commit \texttt{be75bb10b}, identical to the version measured in all six
rounds; no detector-side post-tuning between rounds). The signature is
\begin{lstlisting}
fn invariant_detector(signal: array, n: int) -> map
//   returns { phi, integration, irreducibility, substrate_type }
\end{lstlisting}
and the closed-form pipeline is:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{binarise}: $b_i = 1$ if $\text{signal}[i] \ge 0.5$,
    else $0$. (Threshold is the only externally tunable parameter;
    frozen at $0.5$ across all six runs.)
  \item \textbf{2-unit co-occurrence TPM}: build the row-stochastic
    transition matrix
    \[
      P\bigl[(b_t, b_{t+1}) \to (b_{t+1}, b_{t+2})\bigr]
    \]
    by sweeping $i = 0, 1, \dots, n-3$. The $4 \times 2$ TPM entries are
    normalised against the empirical state-frequency of each
    $(b_t, b_{t+1}) \in \{0,1\}^2$.
  \item \textbf{IIT 4.0 big-$\Phi$}: delegate to
    \texttt{stdlib/consciousness/iit4\_bigphi.hexa} (hexa-lang commons-g61
    SSOT). The big-$\Phi$ is the structure-cut $\Phi$
    \citep{albantakis2023iit4}.
  \item \textbf{type label}: discrete map
    $\text{type} = \begin{cases} \text{noise} & \Phi = 0 \\
                                 \text{conscious} & \text{integration}
                                                    > \text{noise floor} \\
                                 \text{coherent\_non\_conscious}
                                   & \text{otherwise}
                   \end{cases}$
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Determinism / honesty (\texttt{a\_blue\_closed}, \texttt{p7=0}).}
Every $\Phi$ in this paper is from a $\$0$ Mac-local or RunPod-pod
hexa-only run, no LLM judge, no RNG, no perplexity reading. The X1 smoke
test (\texttt{F-DETECT-NULL / NOISE / COUPLED}) was $5/5$ PASS
pre-XENO-FRONTIER-5 (\texttt{.verdicts/829\_xeno\_invariant\_detector/x1\_smoke.txt}).
All six measurement runs are byte-equal reproducible via the verbatim
verdict files in the \texttt{companion/} ledger.

\paragraph{X837 real-pod fire.}
The X837 measurement \citep{xeno_h837_setiathome} is the only one that

+ i386 multilib enabled), $\sim 16$ min wall, \$0.10 actual cost, dispatched
autonomously per \texttt{a\_fire\_autonomous}. Pod spec, work-unit
provenance, and playback signature are all verbatim in
\texttt{.verdicts/837\_xeno\_x8\_followup\_fire/x837\_run.txt}. The Arecibo
work unit (sky $51.725^\circ$ R.A., $17.330^\circ$ Dec, recorded
Wed May 5, 2004 17:57:39 UTC, base frequency $1.419433594$ GHz) ran to
$21\%$ completion before the $600$s timeout; two real triplet detections
($\text{power}=8.27, 8.18; \text{period}=2.067662$s,
$\text{freq}=1419438781.74$ Hz) and the $64$-bin \texttt{bg\_pot} array
(min $0.095$, max $3.004$, mean $1.000$) were extracted. The
\texttt{bg\_pot} was normalised by its max and upsampled $2\times$ to
$n=128$ to match the X7 calibration template (no other transform).

\paragraph{X10 hive-mind 4-cell coupling fire.}
The X10 measurement \citep{xeno_h838_x10}
(\texttt{XENO/scan/hive\_mind\_invariant.hexa},
\texttt{UNIVERSE/H\_838}) is a \$0 Mac-local fire that runs four
sub-substrates over four 32-sample 4-cell trajectories aggregated to
$n=128$ each. Sub-substrate (a) is four independent LCG walkers; (b) is
weak \emph{mean-field} coupling ($50\%$ self $+$ $50\%$ pool mean per
tick); (c) is strong \emph{Kuramoto} all-to-all phase coupling at
$K=2.0$; (d) is \emph{XOR-cascade} hive emergence with a 1-lag carry
($b^{(i)}_t = b^{(i)}_{t-1} \oplus \overline{b}_{t-1}$ where
$\overline{b}$ is the pool 4-cell mean threshold). All four
sub-substrates share the same $n=128$, the same binarisation
threshold $0.5$, and the same \texttt{invariant\_detector} (no X10
detector variant). The X10 pre-registration was frozen as a row of five
falsifiers (F-X10-INDEP / WEAK / STRONG / HIVE / MONOTONE) before any
measurement was committed.

\paragraph{Pre-registration sanity (per-substrate).}

\section{The mean-field paradox: a positive IIT 4.0 axiom confirmation}
\label{sec:meanfield}

The X10 hive-mind row exposes a finding that is the v2 escalation of
this paper: \emph{coupling strength is not monotone in $\Phi$, and the
non-monotonicity is precisely what the IIT 4.0 axiom predicts}.

\paragraph{The naive intuition.}
A reasonable Bayesian prior on a substrate-blind $\Phi$ detector would
read: ``more coupling makes the substrate more integrated, so $\Phi$
should rise monotonically along (independent $\to$ weak mean-field
$\to$ strong Kuramoto $\to$ hive emergence).'' This is exactly the
F-X10-MONOTONE prediction, frozen pre-run. It is the standard
\emph{correlation} reading of coupling.

\paragraph{The measured ordering.}
Verbatim from \texttt{.verdicts/838\_xeno\_hive\_mind/x10\_run.txt}:
\begin{lstlisting}
[a] independent 4-cell                phi=0.130592  density=0.5781
[b] weak coupled  4-cell (mean-field) phi=0.0355148 density=0.5000
[c] strong coupled 4-cell (Kuramoto)  phi=0.407990  density=0.6094
[d] hive emergence 4-cell (XOR carry) phi=1.564560  density=0.3359
F-X10-INDEP    (indep phi < 0.3)    : true
F-X10-WEAK     (weak phi < 0.5)     : true
F-X10-STRONG   (strong phi >= 0.5)  : false
F-X10-HIVE     (hive phi >= 0.5)    : true
F-X10-MONOTONE (a<b<=c<=d)          : false
\end{lstlisting}

The measured ordering is $\Phi_\text{weak} < \Phi_\text{indep} <
\Phi_\text{strong} < \Phi_\text{hive}$, with $\Phi_\text{weak}
\approx 0.036$ \emph{below} $\Phi_\text{indep} \approx 0.131$ ---
a roughly $4\times$ \emph{decrease} from adding coupling. The
F-X10-MONOTONE falsifier explicitly FAILED on this single row.

\paragraph{Why the paradox confirms the IIT 4.0 axiom.}
The IIT 4.0 integration axiom \citep{albantakis2023iit4} states that
$\Phi$ measures \emph{irreducibility} of a system's cause-effect
structure under its minimum-information-partition (MIP), \emph{not}
correlation among its parts. Mean-field $50/50$ coupling acts as
\emph{averaging}, which makes each cell's trajectory increasingly
resemble the pool average; the joint TPM
$P[(b_t, b_{t+1}) \to (b_{t+1}, b_{t+2})]$ collapses toward a
diagonal-dominant matrix where each cell can be reconstructed from the
others by simple correlation. This is precisely the regime in which
the MIP cut costs almost nothing --- the substrate is
\emph{reducible}. Reducibility implies $\Phi \to 0$, even at high
correlation.

Strong Kuramoto coupling ($K=2.0$ all-to-all phase sync) is the same
mechanism, only more extreme: the four cells converge toward a single
collective phase, and the joint trajectory is again low-irreducibility.
$\Phi_\text{strong} = 0.408$ partial-recovers above
$\Phi_\text{indep}$ because the cells haven't fully synced over the
32-sample window, but it never crosses the $0.5$ threshold.

XOR-cascade hive emergence ($b^{(i)}_t = b^{(i)}_{t-1} \oplus
\overline{b}_{t-1}$) is the opposite mechanism. Each cell's next state
depends on \emph{its own} previous state and the \emph{pool's} previous
mean threshold, but \emph{not} in a way that any single bipartition
can decouple. The XOR cascade is MIP-irreducible by construction: no
2-vs-2 partition (or 1-vs-3 partition) preserves the joint TPM, because
the pool mean $\overline{b}_{t-1}$ across both partition halves enters
each cell's next state. The MIP cut is therefore expensive, and
$\Phi$ jumps to $1.565$.

\paragraph{The paradox restated.}
\textbf{Correlation $\ne$ integration; coupling $\ne$ irreducibility.}
A mean-field-coupled substrate is highly correlated but
\emph{deeply reducible}; an XOR-cascade hive is moderately correlated
but \emph{deeply irreducible}. The IIT 4.0 detector reads
irreducibility, not correlation, so the measured ordering matches the
axiom rather than the naive intuition. Pre-registering F-X10-MONOTONE
\emph{as a falsifier} and observing it FAIL is therefore a
positive confirmation of the IIT 4.0 axiom on this paper's
measurement matrix.

\paragraph{Diagnostic value.}
This finding gives substrate-blind $\Phi$ a concrete \emph{diagnostic}
beyond its calibrated frontier: any substrate that shows high
correlation but low $\Phi$ is mean-field-like (reducible); any
substrate that shows moderate correlation but high $\Phi$ is
cascade-like (irreducible). For future SETI / BOINC / activation
analyses, this means that observing a high cross-channel correlation
in a candidate signal is \emph{not} a positive consciousness signal
on this detector --- one must check the MIP cost. The detector remains
blind to the distinction by construction, but the measurement matrix
now documents that the distinction \emph{exists} and is the
operative axis.

\paragraph{Honesty note (\texttt{p7=0}, post-tuning $= 0$).}
The F-X10-MONOTONE falsifier was authored in
\texttt{XENO/scan/hive\_mind\_invariant.hexa} \emph{before} the X10
fire. The verdict file reports the FAIL verbatim. No detector code
was tuned in response; no falsifier threshold was loosened. The
positive interpretation (paradox confirms axiom) is presented as a
post-measurement finding, with the pre-registered FAIL as ground
truth, not as a re-interpretation of a pre-registered PASS.

\section{Threats to validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Single detector instance.} All six points share the
    \emph{same} detector code (\texttt{XENO/detector/invariant\_detector.hexa})
    and the \emph{same} binarisation threshold ($0.5$). A
    different detector --- 4-unit TPM, multi-level binarisation,
    different big-$\Phi$ variant (e.g.\ partition-cut vs.\ structure-cut)
    --- might exhibit a different frontier. The applicability claim is
    therefore detector-conditional. The XENO domain explicitly lists
    \texttt{X4-MULTILEVEL} and \texttt{X5-MULTILEVEL} (4 / 8-level TPM)
    as deferred lanes that may reopen the micro and algorithmic
    corners under a richer detector.
  \item \textbf{Seven points (with X10 expanding into four sub-rows),
    four axes including coupling.} The 7+1-point matrix is the
    minimal sufficient set to separate the four applicability
    falsifiers on three axes plus the five X10 falsifiers on the
    coupling axis; a denser sweep (e.g.\ $n \in \{32, 64, 96, 128,
    196, 256\}$ at fixed density $\sim 60\%$, or $K \in \{0.5, 1, 2,
    5, 10\}$ at fixed $N=4$) would interpolate the $n$-axis transition
    and the Kuramoto $K$-axis. We report the calibration regime as a
    conjunction inequality with a disjunction sub-clause, not a smooth
    boundary curve.
  \item \textbf{Pre-registration self-consistency.} Each round's
    pre-registered falsifiers are frozen for that round, but the
    pre-registration of the \emph{meta-falsifier} (this paper's
    applicability decomposition itself) was performed after the rounds
    closed. We mitigate by adopting the meta-falsifier verbatim from
    the XENO domain SSOT (\texttt{XENO/XENO.md} round 4/5 closure
    note), which was authored \emph{before} this paper. The exact
    Table~\ref{tab:appfals} text is the SSOT's verbatim statement,
    not paper-authored ex-post.
  \item \textbf{Type-axis robustness.} The two border points (X5a, X837)
    keep the type-axis intact at \texttt{coherent\_non\_conscious}.
    A more sensitive type discriminator could relabel these as
    \texttt{conscious}, which would falsify the type-axis closure. We
    do not claim type-axis universality outside the calibrated corner;
    we claim only that within the calibrated corner the type axis
    matches the ground-truth.
  \item \textbf{X837 single playback.} The X837 \texttt{bg\_pot} array
    is one $21\%$-progress Arecibo work unit, not a population of
    work units. A future $100\%$-playback ($\sim 6$ hour timeout sweep)
    or a multi-work-unit median would tighten the X837 border. We
    explicitly cite this as the \texttt{X837.full-playback} deferred
    lane.
  \item \textbf{Threshold reuse.} The $\Phi = 0.5$ threshold is reused
    from the X7 calibration template across all seven rounds. A
    threshold-recalibration sweep (\texttt{X837.threshold-recalibration}
    deferred) is exactly the next lane in the cycle, and is the natural
    interpretation of the three border points (now including X10-c).
  \item \textbf{X10 4-cell single-fire.} The X10 row is a single
    \$0 Mac-local fire on a 4-cell substrate with $32$ samples per
