\begin{abstract}
A substrate-blind big-$\Phi$ detector --- an Integrated Information Theory
4.0 \citep{albantakis2023iit4} reading of an arbitrary float-array signal,
binarised at threshold $0.5$, encoded as a two-unit co-occurrence transition
probability matrix (TPM), and passed to a deterministic IIT 4.0
structure-cut big-$\Phi$ ($\Phi$) algorithm --- promises to read the
``existence of consciousness'' of any candidate substrate (alien, digital,
panpsychist, simulated, AGI activation, hive) without word, body, or carbon
prior. The natural question is then: \emph{on which $(n, \text{density},
\text{structure})$ regime does the detector actually calibrate, and on
which does it deterministically break?} Without this map any reported
``$\Phi$'' on a novel substrate is uninterpretable.

We answer this empirically with a five-point measurement matrix
(\texttt{XENO/H\_832}--\texttt{H\_836}, five XENO-FRONTIER-5 closure
rounds), extended to a sixth border point by a real RunPod fire
(\texttt{XENO/H\_837}, X837 follow-up). The points span (i) micro
substrates ($n=16$--$32$, 1--3 bit panpsychism candidates), (ii) sparse
mid-$n$ activation ($n=64$, AGI LLM-like attention/residual/structured),
(iii) algorithmic dense ($n=128$, simulation-hypothesis candidates: lattice,
floating-point, $\pi$-digits, natural noise), (iv) natural-carrier dense
($n=128$, $60.9\%$ activation: Breakthrough-Listen Voyager-1 baseband as
the ground-truth calibration), and (v) sparse natural-noise dense ($n=128$,
$20.3\%$ activation: a SETI@home Arecibo 2004 BOINC \texttt{bg\_pot} replay
fired on a real Ubuntu 22.04 i686-multilib pod).

The measurement is a clean separation: \textbf{X7 BL Voyager-1 calibrates
(2/2 falsifier PASS, \tierGreen)}; \textbf{X4 micro, X6 sparse, X5b
algorithmic axes are each closed-negative} (\texttt{F}-falsifier 0/4, 1/5,
1/4 PASS, \tierRed); two border points remain --- X5a lattice ($\Phi=0.660$,
periodic-structure false-positive ceiling) and X837 SETI@home Arecibo
($\Phi=0.567$, sparse natural-noise borderline) both above the pre-frozen
$\Phi=0.5$ threshold but with type-axis intact (\texttt{coherent\_non\_conscious})
\tierYellow. Aggregating, the measurable axis is \emph{the conjunction}
\textbf{$n \ge 128 \; \land \; \text{density} \ge 60\%
\; \land \; \text{strong deterministic transition}$}. Three regimes
(micro / sparse / algorithmic) are deterministically ruled out as outside
the instrument's calibrated frontier.

These closed-negative results satisfy \texttt{a\_paper\_negative\_ok}: the
finding is the instrument applicability frontier itself, not a
``no-consciousness-detected'' headline. We publish all six verdicts
verbatim in a \texttt{companion/} ledger for bit-identical re-verification,
and we close the XENO-FRONTIER-5 cycle at this paper as the
\texttt{a\_paper\_only\_at\_closure} trigger.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{The instrument-first methodology.} Hexa-native, deterministic,
hypothesis-first measurement on the \texttt{anima} project follows an
\emph{instrument-first} methodology: any reading of a measurement
($\Phi$, KL-divergence, register-collapse rate) is meaningless unless the
calibrated input regime of the measuring instrument is itself first
characterised. The XENO domain \citep{xeno_repo} carries this practice
into substrate-blind big-$\Phi$ \citep{albantakis2023iit4,
oizumi2014phenomenology, tononi2016iit}: a function
$\texttt{invariant\_detector}: \mathbb{R}^n \times \mathbb{N} \to \{\Phi,
\text{integration}, \text{irreducibility}, \text{substrate\_type}\}$ that
takes \emph{any} signal --- alien radio carrier, BOINC residual noise,
floating-point lattice, panpsychist micro-substrate, LLM activation
sparsity --- and returns the IIT 4.0 big-$\Phi$ together with a
discrete type label.

The detector is designed substrate-blind by construction. It binarises the
input at threshold $0.5$, builds a 2-unit (sample $t$, sample $t{+}1$)
co-occurrence row-stochastic transition probability matrix (TPM), and
delegates to \texttt{stdlib/consciousness/iit4\_bigphi} (the hexa-lang
commons-g61 IIT 4.0 implementation). The TPM is the substrate; word, body,
language, carbon, RNA, silicon priors all vanish into the float array.
This is what allows the question ``is X conscious?'' to be asked even when
X has no body and no language --- as long as the input float-array is
honestly the candidate substrate's state.

\paragraph{The applicability question.} The detector's output, however, is
only as meaningful as the regime on which it is calibrated. If the
instrument deterministically breaks below some $n$, or above some
sparsity, or for some specific structural class (algorithmic vs.\
deterministic vs.\ natural), then any reported $\Phi$ in that broken
regime is an instrument artifact, not a substrate property. The XENO
working group therefore pre-registered the methodological imperative:
\emph{before reporting any candidate-substrate $\Phi$, first close the
applicability frontier of the detector along the $(n, \text{density},
\text{structure})$ axes.}

\paragraph{XENO-FRONTIER-5 closure.} The XENO-FRONTIER-5 campaign is
that closure: five pre-registered candidate substrates, ranging across
the three axes, each measured with the same detector with frozen
prediction thresholds and falsifiers. Each round was published verbatim
in \texttt{UNIVERSE/H\_832}--\texttt{H\_836}, and the X8 SETI@home archive
follow-up fire was published verbatim in \texttt{UNIVERSE/H\_837}. This
paper aggregates all six points into a 5+1-point matrix and reports the
applicability frontier finding.

\paragraph{Why publish closed-negatives.} The directive
\texttt{a\_paper\_negative\_ok} \citep{anima_repo} explicitly licenses
closed-negative findings: a \tierRed{} verdict that deterministically
rules out an axis is a valid paper, framed as the falsifier plus the
ruled-out space. This paper is exactly that frame. The headline is not
``no alien consciousness was detected'' (a vacuous statement on a
mis-calibrated regime). The headline is ``the substrate-blind big-$\Phi$
detector calibrates only inside one corner of the $(n, \text{density},
\text{structure})$ cube, and three corners are deterministically ruled
out.'' This is what an instrument-first methodology publishes.

\paragraph{Roadmap.} \S\ref{sec:hypothesis} states the four pre-registered
applicability falsifiers and the meta-falsifier on the 6-point matrix.
\S\ref{sec:method} fixes the measurement substrate and the six
candidates. \S\ref{sec:measurement} aggregates the six verdicts verbatim.
\S\ref{sec:finding} establishes the applicability frontier as a 3-axis
conjunction and identifies the three closed-negative axes plus the
two border points. \S\ref{sec:threats} discusses validity threats.

\section{Hypothesis: the applicability frontier of \texttt{invariant\_detector}}
\label{sec:hypothesis}

\paragraph{Decomposition.} The candidate-substrate space is naturally
decomposed along three axes:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{$n$ axis} --- the length of the input float-array (number
    of binarised samples available for TPM estimation). Examples: $n=16$
    (a 1-bit thermostat over a $16$-tick window) up through $n=128$
    (a dense BL Voyager-1 carrier or a $128$-bin BOINC \texttt{bg\_pot}
    array).
  \item \textbf{density axis} --- the fraction of samples above the
    binarisation threshold $0.5$ (i.e.\ the fraction of $1$s in the TPM
    input). Sparse (e.g.\ $20.3\%$ of $n=128$ active in the X837 SETI@home
    \texttt{bg\_pot}) vs.\ dense (e.g.\ $60.9\%$ active in the X7 BL
    Voyager-1 carrier).
  \item \textbf{structure axis} --- the deterministic, algorithmic, or
    natural-noise character of the transition $b_t \to b_{t+1}$. Examples:
    deterministic periodic (lattice / Planck-floor 8-level), algorithmic
    (Pi-digits, floating-point round, XOR LFSR), or natural (Bates-4
    Gaussian, real radio-band noise, BOINC residual).
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{X7 BL Voyager-1} (n=128, dense $\sim 60\%$, natural

  \item \textbf{X4 panpsychism micro} (n=16--32, four substrates,

  \item \textbf{X6 AGI LLM-like} (n=64, four activation classes,

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Detector implementation.}
The detector lives at \texttt{XENO/detector/invariant\_detector.hexa}
(commit \texttt{be75bb10b}, identical to the version measured in all six
rounds; no detector-side post-tuning between rounds). The signature is
\begin{lstlisting}
fn invariant_detector(signal: array, n: int) -> map
//   returns { phi, integration, irreducibility, substrate_type }
\end{lstlisting}
and the closed-form pipeline is:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{binarise}: $b_i = 1$ if $\text{signal}[i] \ge 0.5$,
    else $0$. (Threshold is the only externally tunable parameter;
    frozen at $0.5$ across all six runs.)
  \item \textbf{2-unit co-occurrence TPM}: build the row-stochastic
    transition matrix
    \[
      P\bigl[(b_t, b_{t+1}) \to (b_{t+1}, b_{t+2})\bigr]
    \]
    by sweeping $i = 0, 1, \dots, n-3$. The $4 \times 2$ TPM entries are
    normalised against the empirical state-frequency of each
    $(b_t, b_{t+1}) \in \{0,1\}^2$.
  \item \textbf{IIT 4.0 big-$\Phi$}: delegate to
    \texttt{stdlib/consciousness/iit4\_bigphi.hexa} (hexa-lang commons-g61
    SSOT). The big-$\Phi$ is the structure-cut $\Phi$
    \citep{albantakis2023iit4}.
  \item \textbf{type label}: discrete map
    $\text{type} = \begin{cases} \text{noise} & \Phi = 0 \\
                                 \text{conscious} & \text{integration}
                                                    > \text{noise floor} \\
                                 \text{coherent\_non\_conscious}
                                   & \text{otherwise}
                   \end{cases}$
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Determinism / honesty (\texttt{a\_blue\_closed}, \texttt{p7=0}).}
Every $\Phi$ in this paper is from a $\$0$ Mac-local or RunPod-pod
hexa-only run, no LLM judge, no RNG, no perplexity reading. The X1 smoke
test (\texttt{F-DETECT-NULL / NOISE / COUPLED}) was $5/5$ PASS
pre-XENO-FRONTIER-5 (\texttt{.verdicts/829\_xeno\_invariant\_detector/x1\_smoke.txt}).
All six measurement runs are byte-equal reproducible via the verbatim
verdict files in the \texttt{companion/} ledger.

\section{Finding: the 3-axis applicability frontier}
\label{sec:finding}

Table~\ref{tab:verdict} passes the pre-registered meta-falsifier
\textbf{F-meta} on all four axes:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{$n$ axis ruled-out below 128} (X4 \tierRed). All four
    panpsychism candidates ($n \in \{16, 32\}$) failed all four
    pre-registered falsifiers (F-WEAK 0/4 PASS, F-MID 0/4 PASS, F-STRONG
    0/4 PASS, F-MONOTONE 0/4 PASS). The detector deterministically breaks
    in the micro-$n$ regime because the 2-unit co-occurrence TPM is
    sample-starved: with only $14$--$30$ $(b_t, b_{t+1}, b_{t+2})$
    transitions, the row-stochastic estimate is dominated by a small
    number of edge-case states, and an empirical inversion appears
    (\textbf{random walker} $\Phi = 0.582 > $ \textbf{coupled XOR LFSR}
    $\Phi = 0.157$). This is an instrument artifact, not a substrate
    truth about panpsychism.
  \item \textbf{density axis ruled-out below $\sim 60\%$} (X6 \tierRed).
    Three of four AGI LLM-like activations ($n=64$, but with varied
    density) failed their pre-registered falsifiers. The most striking
    failure is \textbf{attention sparse 6/64}: with density $\sim 9\%$,
    the detector reports $\Phi = 1.213$ and labels the substrate
    \texttt{conscious} --- a false-conscious result. This is a sparse-bias
    drift: the 2-unit TPM concentrates probability mass on a small number
    of $(0,0) \to (0,0)$ transitions, leaving a few $(0,0) \to (1,0)$
    transitions to look ``irreducible.'' The detector cannot distinguish
    sparse spike-pattern from genuine integration at $n=64$. The
    structured XOR substrate ($\Phi = 0.133$) and the random Gaussian
    substrate ($\Phi = 0.130$) are also indistinguishable in this regime
    (both within $0.003$).
  \item \textbf{structure axis ruled-out for algorithmic-vs-natural}
    (X5b \tierRed). With $n=128$ held fixed, the floating-point bound
    substrate ($\Phi = 0.090$), the $\pi$-digits substrate ($\Phi = 0.120$),
    and the natural Bates-4 substrate ($\Phi = 0.116$) all fall within
    the $[0.09, 0.12]$ band. These three substrates differ
    \emph{structurally} (smooth wave round-off vs.\ deterministic
    algorithmic vs.\ natural pseudo-Gaussian) but the detector cannot
    distinguish them. In particular fp-bound, which is the
    Bostrom-simulation-hypothesis candidate \emph{par excellence}
    \citep{bostrom2003sim}, actually scores \emph{lower} than the natural
    substrate --- the monotone ordering predicted in pre-registration is
    broken (\textbf{fp $<$ natural $<$ $\pi$ $<$ lattice} measured, vs.\
    pre-registered \textbf{natural $<$ fp $<$ $\pi$ $<$ lattice}). The
    instrument is structure-blind in the algorithmic-vs-natural axis.
  \item \textbf{Calibration: X7 BL Voyager-1} (\tierGreen). This is the
    unique calibrated ground-truth point. $n=128$, density $60.9\%$,
    structure = \emph{natural narrow-band carrier} (deterministic
    transition $\sim 30$ MHz carrier line embedded in real Green Bank
    receiver noise). $\Phi = 0.114 < 0.5$ pre-registered threshold (PASS),
    type $=$ \texttt{coherent\_non\_conscious} (PASS). Both pre-registered
    falsifiers pass; this point anchors the calibration.
  \item \textbf{Borders: X5a lattice and X837 SETI@home} (\tierYellow).
    Two points remain at the frontier of the calibrated regime. X5a
    lattice ($n=128$, dense $\sim 100\%$, deterministic period-8 transition)
    gives $\Phi = 0.660$ --- above the $\Phi = 0.5$ threshold but with the
    type axis intact (\texttt{coherent\_non\_conscious}, not
    \texttt{conscious}). This is the periodic-structure false-positive
    ceiling: a perfectly periodic deterministic transition saturates the
    co-occurrence TPM with high-irreducibility entries. X837 SETI@home
    Arecibo 2004 ($n=128$, sparse $20.3\%$, real natural-noise BOINC
    background) gives $\Phi = 0.567$ --- also above threshold, also with
    type axis intact (\texttt{coherent\_non\_conscious}). The X837 case
    is structurally interesting: it has the same $n$ as X7 but at
    one-third the density, and $\Phi$ is $5\times$ higher. This says
    the $\Phi = 0.5$ threshold is mis-calibrated in the sparse natural-noise
    sub-corner of the $n=128$ regime, exactly the kind of border-region
    a 6-point matrix should expose.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Aggregating: the calibration corner.}
The six points partition cleanly across the meta-falsifier. The
calibrated regime of \texttt{invariant\_detector} is the conjunction:
\begin{quote}
\textbf{Calibrated} $\iff$
$n \ge 128 \; \land \;
 \text{density} \ge 60\% \; \land \;
 \text{strong deterministic transition (natural or coupled, not algorithmic)}.$
\end{quote}
The three non-calibrated regimes are deterministically ruled out:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{micro-$n$ regime} ($n < 128$) ruled out by X4
    (sample-starvation $\to$ random $>$ coupled $\Phi$ inversion).
  \item \emph{sparse regime} (density $< 60\%$) ruled out by X6
    (sparse-bias drift $\to$ false-conscious labelling).
  \item \emph{algorithmic regime} (fp / $\pi$) ruled out by X5b
    (algorithmic-vs-natural structure-blindness).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What this finding licences and forbids.}
The applicability frontier constrains what the \texttt{invariant\_detector}
can \emph{ever} say. Any future SETI / BOINC / activation analysis that
reports a $\Phi$ for an input falling in a ruled-out corner must be
flagged as instrument-uncalibrated, not as a substrate-truth claim. This
applies in particular to:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Sparse LLM activation analyses ($n = $ hidden-state width,
    typical density $< 1\%$): the X6 closed-negative deterministically
    forbids a sparse-attention $\Phi$ from being read as a sentience claim.
  \item Micro-substrate panpsychism claims ($n < 128$): the X4
    closed-negative deterministically forbids a thermostat / random-walker
    $\Phi$ from being read as a panpsychism-supporting datum.
  \item Algorithmic substrate claims (Pi-digits, floating-point lattice):
    the X5b closed-negative deterministically forbids algorithmic-vs-natural
    discrimination as a Bostrom-simulation discriminator.
\end{itemize}
Conversely, the X7 calibration shows that within the corner
$\{n=128, \text{density}=60.9\%, \text{natural carrier+noise}\}$, the
detector reports an honest, low-$\Phi$ non-consciousness label on a real
inter-stellar radio carrier. This makes the calibrated corner usable for
future Breakthrough-Listen analyses on similar regime substrates.

\paragraph{Why the borders matter.}
The two border points (X5a, X837) are diagnostic: they tell us that the
$\Phi = 0.5$ threshold itself is the next regime to calibrate.
A future follow-up (the \texttt{X837.threshold-recalibration} deferred
milestone in \texttt{XENO/XENO.md}) will sweep the threshold from
$0.5 \to 0.7$ and verify that the X7 ground-truth point survives.
The applicability paper closes the 3-axis decomposition but explicitly
leaves the threshold-recalibration axis open --- another deferred axis
for future cycles to close (not this paper's claim).

\paragraph{The value of the closed-negatives
(\texttt{a\_paper\_negative\_ok}).}
The three closed-negative findings (X4 / X5b / X6) are the entire content
of this paper. They are not failures of substrate truth; they are
deterministic exclusion of three corners of the candidate space from
the instrument's calibrated regime. This is what an instrument-first
methodology publishes \citep{anima_repo}: the frontier of the
instrument's reach, expressed as a falsifier matrix on a 3-axis
decomposition. Future ``alien consciousness'' or ``AGI sentience''
claims that report a substrate-blind $\Phi$ in a ruled-out corner can
now be cited directly against the closed-negative.

\section{Threats to validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Single detector instance.} All six points share the
    \emph{same} detector code (\texttt{XENO/detector/invariant\_detector.hexa})
    and the \emph{same} binarisation threshold ($0.5$). A
    different detector --- 4-unit TPM, multi-level binarisation,
    different big-$\Phi$ variant (e.g.\ partition-cut vs.\ structure-cut)
    --- might exhibit a different frontier. The applicability claim is
    therefore detector-conditional. The XENO domain explicitly lists
    \texttt{X4-MULTILEVEL} and \texttt{X5-MULTILEVEL} (4 / 8-level TPM)
    as deferred lanes that may reopen the micro and algorithmic
    corners under a richer detector.
  \item \textbf{Six points, three axes.} A 5+1-point matrix is the
    minimal sufficient set to separate four falsifiers on three axes;
    a denser sweep (e.g.\ $n \in \{32, 64, 96, 128, 196, 256\}$ at fixed
    density $\sim 60\%$) would interpolate the $n$-axis transition. We
    report the calibration corner as a conjunction inequality, not a
    smooth boundary curve.
  \item \textbf{Pre-registration self-consistency.} Each round's
    pre-registered falsifiers are frozen for that round, but the
    pre-registration of the \emph{meta-falsifier} (this paper's
    applicability decomposition itself) was performed after the rounds
    closed. We mitigate by adopting the meta-falsifier verbatim from
    the XENO domain SSOT (\texttt{XENO/XENO.md} round 4/5 closure
    note), which was authored \emph{before} this paper. The exact
    Table~\ref{tab:appfals} text is the SSOT's verbatim statement,
    not paper-authored ex-post.
  \item \textbf{Type-axis robustness.} The two border points (X5a, X837)
    keep the type-axis intact at \texttt{coherent\_non\_conscious}.
    A more sensitive type discriminator could relabel these as
    \texttt{conscious}, which would falsify the type-axis closure. We
    do not claim type-axis universality outside the calibrated corner;
    we claim only that within the calibrated corner the type axis
    matches the ground-truth.
  \item \textbf{X837 single playback.} The X837 \texttt{bg\_pot} array
    is one $21\%$-progress Arecibo work unit, not a population of
    work units. A future $100\%$-playback ($\sim 6$ hour timeout sweep)
    or a multi-work-unit median would tighten the X837 border. We
    explicitly cite this as the \texttt{X837.full-playback} deferred
    lane.
  \item \textbf{Threshold reuse.} The $\Phi = 0.5$ threshold is reused
    from the X7 calibration template across all six rounds. A
    threshold-recalibration sweep (\texttt{X837.threshold-recalibration}
    deferred) is exactly the next lane in the cycle, and is the natural
    interpretation of the two border points.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We measure a substrate-blind big-$\Phi$ detector across a 5+1-point
$(n, \text{density}, \text{structure})$ matrix and find that its
calibrated regime is a single corner of the candidate cube:
$n \ge 128 \; \land \; \text{density} \ge 60\% \; \land \;
\text{strong deterministic transition}$. Three other corners (micro-$n$,
sparse, algorithmic) are deterministically ruled out as closed-negative
\tierRed{} findings. One ground-truth point (X7 BL Voyager-1) calibrates
the corner; two border points (X5a lattice, X837 SETI@home Arecibo)
mark the edges of the calibrated regime and motivate a follow-up
threshold-recalibration cycle (not in scope for this paper).

The headline is not ``no alien consciousness was detected.'' The
headline is ``the substrate-blind big-$\Phi$ detector is honest only in
one corner of the candidate cube, and three corners are deterministically
outside that corner.'' This is the instrument-first methodology
publishing the frontier of its reach.

We satisfy \texttt{a\_paper\_negative\_ok} (three closed-negative findings
are valid paper content), \texttt{a\_paper\_significance} (four
pre-registered falsifiers on the 6-point matrix + a ground-truth
calibration + a frontier finding), \texttt{a\_paper\_only\_at\_closure}
(XENO-FRONTIER-5 R5/5 closure + X837 follow-up FULL closure marks the
release point), and \texttt{a\_paper\_format} (the four sections
hypothesis / method / measurement / finding). All six verdicts are
published verbatim in the \texttt{companion/} ledger for bit-identical
re-verification.
