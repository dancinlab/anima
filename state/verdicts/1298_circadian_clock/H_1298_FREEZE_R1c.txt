THE PHASE-LOCKING METRIC (vector strength R, the standard circadian regularity measure):
Given the fire ticks {f_1 < f_2 < ... < f_k} produced by an arm over the run, map each
fire onto the unit circle at angle theta_i = 2*pi*(f_i mod PERIOD)/PERIOD and take the
mean resultant length:
       R = | (1/k) * sum_i exp(i*theta_i) |     in [0,1]
A clock that fires at a CONSISTENT phase (always phase-0) → all theta_i identical → R=1.0
(perfectly phase-locked). A schedule whose fires are spread across phases (shuffle,
ablate-with-non-dividing-period, or a homeostat that fires irregularly/not at all) → the
unit vectors cancel → R near 0 (chance for k vectors is ~1/sqrt(k), small). An arm that
NEVER fires (k=0, the constant-grounded homeostat) → R defined as 0.0 (no phase lock).
This is exactly the measure chronobiology uses to quantify circadian rhythmicity, and it
collapses BY CONSTRUCTION for any non-periodic firing pattern — the metric chance is ~0.
