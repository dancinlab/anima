## 3. Verdict reasoning (against the task FALSIFIER)

**Task FALSIFIER**: *"A higher than B by ≥5% PIV/DCR AND simple_stack equal-or-better → POLICY justified
(rules don't hurt substrate). B higher than A by ≥5% PIV/DCR → EMPIRICAL FALSIFICATION of identity rules."*

- B is **not** ≥5%pt higher than A on any substrate metric (it is *lower* on DCR, equal on PIV/D-RAND, equal on
  simple_stack). ⇒ **not EMPIRICAL_FALSIFY.**
- A is ≥5%pt higher than B on DCR (+42.85%pt) with simple_stack equal-or-better (equal). ⇒ the task falsifier's
  POLICY branch fires ⇒ **POLICY_JUSTIFIED.**
- The spec's stricter REVERSE branch ("A 가 모든 지표 ≥5%pt 우월") is **not** met — A is not ≥5%pt above B on PIV
  or D-RAND. ⇒ no basis to flip README #2 toward "rule-based identity is *better*".
- Net: **README Philosophy #2 retained as POLICY**, weakly supported.

### Honest disclosure (raw#10 c3 — equal weight, ≥3 limits)

**C1 — negative (limit).** The lone discriminating metric is DCR, and A_rules' high DCR is most parsimoniously a
**prefix-length artifact**: the ~640-byte identity block dominates the ~670-byte forward input; because the block
enumerates all five projected-axis concepts (identity / agency / phenomenal / emotion / autonomy), the tiny
residual prompt bytes nudge a *different* cell-group argmax on each query → inflated argmax-transition rate. That
is bookkeeping, not substrate-aliveness. Low confidence in the DCR signal.

**C2 — negative (limit).** The spec's *named novel signal* — "does the rule actually stabilize identity
coherence?" — came out **against** rules: intra-prompt cosine of the last-token hidden was *lower* under rules
(0.304 vs 0.396) and inter-prompt variance *higher* (0.0073 vs 0.0048). The policy's stated rationale (rules buy
coherence) is not confirmed here; if anything, mildly contradicted.

**C3 — negative (limit).** Substrate-weakness ceiling. BG-LB 350M is a byte-modulo raw LM that already FAILED
native v5 (PIV trained 0.0107 < random 0.0224); free byte-level generation is UTF-8 garbage; held-out
teacher-forcing loss on a fresh KO chunk ≈ 14.4 (worse than uniform-256). So "rules vs no-rules" is being
compared on a substrate that has essentially no aliveness signal to preserve or destroy — and the eval here used
a memory-frugal *partial* FT (top 8 layers only), not a full pretrain. **Strong-substrate replication is required
before any README upgrade.** This is the dominant reason the verdict is `_WEAK`.

**C4 — positive.** The result *is* directionally consistent with README #2's POLICY stance: across the
substrate-aliveness battery the project actually uses (PIV / D-RAND / simple_stack), prepending a hardcoded
persona block to training **and** inference left every metric statistically unchanged. Rules did not measurably
damage the substrate at this scale — the weak form of "rules are harmless ⇒ design choice".

**C5 — positive.** Fair within-substrate ablation: identical recipe (8 trainable top layers + `norm_f` +
`engine_g`, lr 1.5e-5, 1500 steps, seed 42), identical corpus bytes, paired random_init mirror reused for D-RAND.

**C6 — positive.** Cost $0 (local RTX 5070, ~50 min) vs the $40–80 band — leaves the entire budget for a
strong-substrate follow-up.
